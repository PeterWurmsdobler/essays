AI Helps Reaching a Safe Operating Space within Planetary Boundaries

AI can be a means for helping humanity to return and stay in a safe operating space within planetary boundaries.

My previous posts on AI tools such as AI Tools in Software Development — The Next Era for its Growth Rate are rather positive takes on AI and AI-tools; they are techno-optimistic accounts and show perhaps how engineers tend to be carried away by purely technical aspects. However, a wider context has to be considered, in space and time, given the Planetary Boundaries [1] and a finite planet; this essay elaborates on my position with regards to AI being a means to an end, helping humanity to return and stay in a safe operating space within planetary boundaries.

Figure 1. AI and AI tools being helpers to find ways for returning and staying within Planetary Boundaries [Collage-1].

As an engineer, I have been attracted to technology all my life, perhaps because its implicit reductionism allows fitting complicated machines into manageable models which in turn create more such systems (natural systems, including social and economic systems, are in contrast complex and cannot easily be reduced without losing fidelity). As such I have worked in various domains (industrial automation, radar systems, autonomous driving) where the technical aspect was interesting to me. Nevertheless, the societal context has always played an important role, too, in particular the ultimate purpose my effort contributed. In fact, quite often the purpose drove my career choices, e.g. joining Five AI to work on shared mobility, reducing the number of cars needed to a fraction; the autonomous driving aspect tickled my curious mind.

Over the years I have acquired knowledge in software development, using various programming languages and tools. Software to me was and still remains a means to an end; it helps solving concrete real-world problems, e.g. making a motor turn. My work led me to AI-tools as well in a wider sense, from early Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Fuzzy Logic for control, later Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) in object detection and in more recent days, using AI tools such as Microsoft Copilot as a programming aid. I see AI that way: a tool to take away tedium, automate repetitive tasks, and above all, find solutions where my knowledge is limited (everywhere); to me they are not a threat, rather an opportunity.

That said, I do not worry too much about AI; it is ubiquitous. Certainly we are at the moment on a hype with a lot of investment (proportional to the hope on returns and expectations) which will come to an end at some point. This cycle reminds me of many others in the past, the ‘turbo’ era in the 1980s, or the ‘Fuzzy Logic’ times, even the autonomous driving hype a couple of years ago. On the flip side, past the hype, a residual, more sane amount of investment and expectations remains; that is still worth having, and I believe that is true for AI, too: viewing AI as a powerful tool that can be very helpful in many domains, including finding solutions to the challenges of climate change, as only one dimension where our civilisation is breaching the safe limits of planetary boundaries.

Humanity, in particular the established economic system which did indeed bring prosperity to large parts of the world through the exploitation of fossil fuels, has manoeuvred itself into a bit of a pickle: unbridled growth will bring the civilisation to an end due to either pollution or resource depletion, or changing the economic system all of a sudden will result in a societal collapse. My take at the moment is that a staged transition is required with three prongs being phased in at different times:

  • Comprehensive technology for short term changes now,
  • System change towards ecological economics mid term, and
  • Stabilised system à la Well-being Economy long term.

As the system stands at the moment, most efforts should go into comprehensive technology, but must not be seen as the ultimate goal, only as a stepping stone while a more systemic change — even a controlled economic contraction to a sustainable state — becomes more acceptable. In this first phase, given the urgency of action to recover the planet’s dire situation, many measures are necessary in parallel, no single solution exists, all means are fine, AI being one of them. AI can try unconventional combinations, pull various aspects together, explore a very high-dimensional parameter space a human would not be able to do, e.g. in material science to come up with a battery chemistry no one dared to think about. Though, no miracles expected or nuclear fusion in your garden shed.

There is a cost of course, like material and energy footprint of AI; however, similar to the concept of Energy Return on Energy Invested (ERoEI), I do hope that a) the ERoEI is considerably larger than one, and b) the effort invested in AI will pay off by producing technology that can help humanity in the transition to a more sustainable existence, truly sustainable, paraphrasing David Attenborough: ‘only things that can be done in perpetuity’. In fact, I trust that AI won’t come up with a trivial solution, eliminate humanity, but with one in the spirit of Gaya Herrington’s pledge for an ‘enough for all’ society.
